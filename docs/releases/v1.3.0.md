Logsift 1.3 explains heap dumps and GC logs, and is careful around production: every action on a running JVM says what it costs, and Logsift never removes files on a server.

## What's new

- **Heap dumps explained:** open a `.hprof` and Logsift says in plain sentences what fills the memory and what holds it. **Why is it alive?** shows the chain from a GC root to any object, and **Map** shows the heap as boxes as big as what they hold. Wasted memory (duplicate strings, empty collections) is found too. Big dumps open with the app's usual memory: a 12 GB dump in about 2½ minutes.
- **Two heap dumps compared:** what grew between them, and where it is kept.
- **Heap dumps on a server:** read a dump where it is, over SSH, on a Linux or macOS server (only the answers come over), or copy it to another machine first. Logsift says what it takes before anything starts.
- **From an OutOfMemoryError to its heap dump:** right-click the line and the dump the JVM wrote opens.
- **GC logs explained:** open a Java GC log (Java 8 to 25; G1, Parallel, Serial, CMS, ZGC, Shenandoah) and **Analyze GC** says how much of the time the program stood still, the longest pauses and why, whether memory after GC keeps growing, and what to try. Charts zoom together; a click shows the line.
- **Safe around production:** each action on a running JVM says what it costs the service, and the README lists them all. Heap dumps and recordings left on a server stay there, readable only by your user, and Logsift says where. Heap dumps check for free space first.
- **Heap dumps over a JMX port:** the JVM writes the dump on its machine, and Logsift offers to copy it here over SSH.
- **Find bar** (Ctrl+F) at the top of the log, **a bar for each log** with its filters as chips, **Clear All**, and **Find All** within the filtered lines.
- **Zips and folders:** find in every file of a zip; folder search results can be filtered by file name, expanded and collapsed.
- **Start page** with everything you can open, and no toolbar on top.
- **Fixed:** attaching over a JMX port in the installed app, empty rows in some CSV files, and downloads behind a company proxy.
